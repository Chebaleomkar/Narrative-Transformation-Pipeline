The Tragedy of Karna — The Unsung Hero of the Mahabharata

Karna, born to Kunti (mother of the Pandavas) and the Sun God Surya before her marriage, was abandoned at birth in a basket on the river. Found and raised by a charioteer couple, Adhiratha and Radha, he grew up with the stigma of being "suta-putra" (son of a charioteer) despite his innate nobility and immense warrior skills.

Rejected by Drona for archery training because of his caste, Karna learned from Parashurama by disguising himself as a Brahmin. When his true identity was revealed—betrayed by his endurance of pain when a bug bore into his thigh while his guru slept on his lap—Parashurama cursed him to forget his most potent weapon (Brahmastra) at his moment of greatest need.

Humiliated at a public tournament where the Pandava prince Arjuna shone, Karna was befriended by Duryodhana, the Kaurava prince. Duryodhana crowned him King of Anga solely to make him eligible to challenge Arjuna. This act of friendship bound Karna to Duryodhana for life, leading him to side with the Kauravas in the great Kurukshetra war, even against his own brothers, the Pandavas.

Before the war, Lord Indra, disguised as a beggar, asked for Karna's natural armor (Kavacha) and earrings (Kundala), which made him invincible. Known for his indiscriminate charity (Dana-Vira), Karna cut them off his own body and gave them away, weakening himself but keeping his vow of charity.

Kunti, his biological mother, finally revealed the truth of his birth to him, pleading for him to join the Pandavas. Karna refused to betray his friend Duryodhana but promised not to kill any Pandava except Arjuna.

On the 17th day of the war, battling Arjuna, Karna's chariot wheel got stuck in the mud (a result of another curse). As he dismounted to lift it, forgetting his mantras as cursed, Arjuna, urged by Krishna, shot the defenseless Karna. He died not because he lacked skill, but because fate and his own rigid code of honor conspired against him.

Key characters: Karna (tragic hero, loyal friend, charitable soul), Duryodhana (antagonist/friend, upholder of Karna's dignity), Arjuna (rival, unsuspecting brother), Kunti (mother who abandoned him), Krishna (divine manipulator of fate), Parashurama (guru who cursed him).

Key themes: Fate vs. Free Will, Loyalty vs. Righteousness, Caste and Identity, The cost of Charity, Friendship above Blood, The tragedy of unrecognized merit.
