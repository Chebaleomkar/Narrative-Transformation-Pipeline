King Vikramaditya and the Vetala — Results of Action

King Vikramaditya, the legendary wise ruler of Ujjain, is tasked by a tantric sorcerer to bring him a Vetala (a vampire-like spirit) hanging from a specific tree in a cremation ground. The task requires absolute silence; if Vikram speaks, the Vetala will fly back to the tree.

Every time Vikram captures the Vetala and slings it over his shoulder, the spirit tells him a story. Each story ends with a complex moral or legal riddle. The Vetala challenges Vikram: "If you know the answer and do not speak, your head will burst into a thousand pieces." Bound by his duty to truth and wisdom, Vikram answers correctly every time.

Example Riddle: A queen and her daughter, lost in the woods, marry a King and his son respectively. The King marries the daughter, and the son marries the mother. They all have children. How are the children related?

As soon as Vikram answers, breaking his silence, the Vetala flies back to the tree. This cycle repeats twenty-four times.

On the twenty-fifth attempt, the Vetala tells a story that stumps even the wise King. Vikram remains silent, not knowing the answer. pleased with his humility and persistence, the Vetala reveals the sorcerer's true plot: to sacrifice Vikram to the goddess Kali after he delivers the spirit.

Forewarned, Vikram outwits the sorcerer. When asked to bow before the goddess, Vikram claims he doesn't know how and asks the sorcerer to demonstrate. As the sorcerer bows, Vikram beheads him, saving himself.

Key characters: King Vikramaditya (symbol of wisdom, persistence, and courage), The Vetala (cunning spirit, riddler, tester of character), The Sorcerer (manipulative antagonist seeking power).

Key themes: The burden of wisdom, Persistence in the face of failure, Wit vs. Brute Force, The nature of Justice, Duty vs. Survival.
